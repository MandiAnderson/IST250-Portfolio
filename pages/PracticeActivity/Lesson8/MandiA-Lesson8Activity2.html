Bearded Vultures
Fact Sheet
Genus Gypaetus barbatus
IUCN Red List Status Near Threatened
Left in the wild 1,675 - 6,700 mature individuals worldwide (BirdLife International 2021)
Breeding pairs 465 breeding pairs in Europe
Breeding behaviour Usually monogamous pairings but trios occur
Range Pyrenees, Sierra Nevada and Alpine mountain range, Corsica and Crete (Europe)
Habitat Remote, mountainous areas, with precipitous terrain, usually above 1,000 m
Size 37 - 49 inches
Wingspan 7 ft 7 in - 9 ft 3 in
Weight 10 - 16.5 pounds
Diet Feeding on carrion, with a large proportion (around 85%) made up of bones, usually of mountain herbivores such Ibex, Chamois, Red Deer and domestic animals like sheep
Life expectancy up to 30 in the wild, up to 50 years in captivity

Vultures play a crucial role in our ecosystem. As scavengers, they eat dead animals, removing them from roadsides and other areas.

They not only cut down on the number of rotten, smelly carcasses but also reduce the spread of diseases.

A world without vultures would be a foul-smelling place filled with disease and rotting carcasses.

Bearded Vultures are the only known vertebrates that can digest bones. They have a highly acidic stomach that allows them to break down and extract nutrients from bones, which make up a significant portion of their diet. This unique adaptation allows them to thrive in their mountainous habitats where carrion is often scarce.
