About CoZara

"Japanese food in the U.S. has to be updated," says Tanaka, who has done so much to upgrade the sushi scene with creative maki artistry at Zama, his restaurant hit near Rittenhouse Square.

There's much more to Japanese food than raw fish. Hiroyuki "Zama" Tanaka was determined to make that point at CoZara in University City. Along with sushi, Zama fired up the grill and deep-fryer to conjure some of his favorite homeland comforts for an izakaya-style gastropub.

CoZara excels in simple Japanese comforts done right - a griddled rice ball lacquered in teriyaki, a hearty braised beef and potato Niku Jaga stew, the thick chunks of velvety white salt-braised pork belly posed over dark ponzu. There is something so soul-satisfying about the purity of the ochazuke, a chunk of broiled salmon over rice that almost turned to congee when your server pours dashi broth from a teapot overtop.

Chef Zama standing inside CoZara

Tanaka opened the 140-seat CoZara in April 2014 next to Zavino and Shake Shack in a commercial strip on Drexel University’s campus. There are many dining options on the two levels: the second-floor main dining room overlooking Chestnut Street, at the counter of the 10-seat open kitchen, at the nine-seat bar by the front door (stocked with Japanese beer and cocktails), and upstairs in a private room. A 30-seat outdoor terrace on the second floor is also an option in warm weather.

About Zama

Hiroyuki "Zama" Tanaka is a renowned chef and restaurateur known for his innovative approach to Japanese cuisine. Zama was born and raised in Kanagawa Japan, in 1971. He came to Philadelphia in 1991 to study English at Temple University through its campus in Japan. He needed a part-time job, and applied at Genji, then a popular Japanese restaurant on Sansom Street near Rittenhouse Square. Tanaka had kitchen experience back home, true — though it was frying burgers and baking pizza for American soldiers at Camp Zama, an Army base near his home in Kanagawa Prefecture. Tanaka climbed the ranks at Genji, heading its sushi and sake counter in the old Ritz-Carlton Hotel, before heading to Fish on Main in Manayunk. He rose through the Starr organization at Pod and Morimoto before opening Zama in 2009 at 128 S. 19th St. to stellar reviews. The Inquirer’s Craig LaBan called Zama “the best all-around Japanese restaurant to open since Morimoto.” Zama restaurant became a Philadelphia institution, frequented by multitudinous sushi lovers, sports celebrities, and Hollywood stars.

Zama was a venerable, self-taught sushi chef and a fixture in Philadelphia’s restaurant community. Many chefs working now carry with them the stamp of Zama’s generosity in sharing his expertise. Zama was known for his creativity and artistry in sushi making, and his restaurants were a beloved destination for sushi lovers in Philadelphia. Zama passed away in 2024, but his legacy lives on through the chefs he mentored and the impact he had on the Philadelphia restaurant scene.

Host An Event Here

CoZara offers diners two floors with a wide variety of dining options including a 10-seat open kitchen, nine seat bar as well as the geisha room, an upstairs private room.

Designed by Boxwood Architects, the restaurant has a chic relaxed atmosphere with varying textures of gray, blonde wooden tables and black iron chairs. Red accents and hand painted images of Shoguns and Geisha serve as the only adornment. Modern red silk moire drum lighting fixtures cast a dim glow. Ceilings are exposed industrial black duct work.

A 30 seat outdoor terrace is also available in the summer months. Our private dining manager is happy to assist you with planning the details of your event.
