What Can We Do?

“If you think you're too small to have an impact, try going to bed with a mosquito in the room.”
~Anita Roddick


The feeling that one person cannot have an impact on the "bigger picture" is not uncommon, however, anyone can make a significant impact.
Listed below are ways that you can be more involved in wolf conservation efforts.


Support AZA-accredited Zoos and Aquariums

The Association of Zoos and Aquariums (AZA) is a 501(c)3 non-profit organization dedicated conservation, education, science, and recreation across zoos and aquariums. There are over 254 AZA-accredited facilities in the United States and overseas that meet the highest standards in animal care and welfare, and provide a fun educational experience. Supporting these accredited zoos and aquariums is one way to help ensure the survival of endangered species. You can learn more about the Association of Zoos and Aquariums (AZA) on their website.

Raise awareness in your community about the importance of wolves as apex predators and keystone species, being mindful of how you do so

Public eduation is crucial for reducing misconceptions about wolves. Without advocating for stronger legal protections, habitat restoration, sustainable livestock practices, and climate action, these threats could reverse conservation efforts and push wolves back to endangerment. Wolves are legally protected under national and international conservation frameworks in many regions, including the Endangered Species Act. Although legal protection has contributed to the recovery of wolf populations in certain areas, they continue to face culling programs and legal hunting quotas due to different jurisductions and lack of legal protections in other regions.


Help with education programs about wildlife and ecology

Look for conservation opportunities and donate your time locally. The U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service has an entire page on their website dedicated to helping people find volunteer opportunities for them. You can find this resource on their website.


Stay up-to-date and informed on your state's open comment periods regarding conservation policy changes

Regularly check websites for your state's environmental protection agency for regulations, public hearings, and comment periods. Public meetings update the public on the current status of local projects and programs and to get public input on proposals in order for the Department to choose and appropriate coarse of action. These meetings are often held virtually and in person. A public comment period is an opportunity to share your opinion on a proposed federal rule or regulation. Public comments are a great way to engage with federal agencies on important decisions.


Advocate for cohabitation between people and wildlife

Though other issues exist that negatively impact wolf populations, a significant threat wolves contend with are humans. The history between humans and wolves is heavily influenced by ignorance and fear. Wolf habitats have been converted to grazing land for cattle, which escalates conflicts. Humans are hunting wolf prey species, increasing the probability that wolves would turn to alternative food sources such as livestock. Consequently, wolves that impede on private property or threaten livestock are hunted. Unfortunately, greater interest lies with the livestock industry than it does for the conservation of wolves, with kill permits often issued when requested.

Wolves are risk-adverse and naturally wary of humans because of past persecution by people. In order to ensure the success of long-term wolf conservation, there needs to be collaborative efforts between communities and conservationists. There are many conservation groups that collaborate with ranchers and farmers to implement tactics to reduce human-wolf conflicts, including non-lethal predator deterrants, livestock guardian dogs, electric fencing, and range riders.

Take Action to Protect the Endangered Species Act

Ask your Congressional representatives to protect our nation's most effective law for protecting wildlife in danger of extinction.


Tell the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service to Protect Gray Wolves

The Service wants to reinstate a rule that stripped protections for gray wolves across the lower 48 states and led to the slaughter of hundreds of wolves.


Tell Congress to Oppose Efforts to Derail Mexican Wolf Recovery

The House Natural Resources Committee advanced a bill that would prematurely remove Endangered Species Act protections for Mexican gray wolves.


VOTE NO on Anti-Wolf Bill H.R. 845

The bill directs the Secretary of the Interior to delist the gray wolf from the Endangered Species Act.
